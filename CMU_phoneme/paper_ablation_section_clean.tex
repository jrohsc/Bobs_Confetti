\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering

\label{tab:ablation}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Configuration} & \textbf{Mean $\Phi_{\text{agg}}$} & \textbf{$\Delta$} \\
\midrule
Full (all 7)    & 0.71 & --- \\
$-S_{st}$       & 0.68 & $-0.03$ \\
$-S_{sy}$       & 0.68 & $-0.03$ \\
$-S_{cv}$       & 0.71 & $-0.00$ \\
$-S_{vow}$      & 0.72 & $+0.01$ \\

To validate that each component of $\Phi_{\text{agg}}$ contributes to measuring phonetic similarity, we conduct an ablation study removing one component at a time. We evaluate on 7 songs spanning four genres: pop (Let It Be, Can't Help Falling in Love), rap (Lose Yourself, HUMBLE, DNA), rock (We Will Rock You), and K-pop (Gangnam Style). For each song, we compute $\Phi_{\text{agg}}$ between original and APT variant lyrics with the target component removed (weight set to zero).

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows that removing syllable count ($S_{sy}$) or stress pattern ($S_{st}$) causes the largest decrease in $\Phi_{\text{agg}}$, indicating these prosodic features contribute most to capturing phonetic similarity relevant to musical text-setting. This aligns with the constraint that syllables map to notes and stressed syllables fall on strong beats. The structural features ($S_{jac}$, $S_{ph}$) show positive $\Delta$ when removed, suggesting they may introduce noise for some word pairs; however, they capture complementary information for edge cases where other metrics fail.

All 7 APT attacks achieved melody similarity $\geq 0.80$ as measured by AudioJudge (mean: 0.90, range: 0.85--0.95), demonstrating that high $\Phi_{\text{agg}}$ correlates with successful melodic memorization attacks.
